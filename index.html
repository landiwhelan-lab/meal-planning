Smoked Mackerel Scrambled Eggs with Cucumber & Tomato
~28 g protein

Ingredients (1 serving)

  • 2 large eggs
  • 20 ml lactose-free milk
  • 80 g smoked mackerel (from fridge — use now), flaked
  • 5 ml olive oil
  • Cucumber slices + 2–3 slices tomato
  • Black pepper · fresh chives (fridge)

Method

  1. Whisk eggs with milk, season well with black pepper.
  2. Heat olive oil in a non-stick pan over low-medium heat. Add eggs. Stir slowly with a spatula — low and slow makes them creamy.
  3. Just before fully set, fold in flaked smoked mackerel. Remove from heat — residual heat finishes them.
  4. Serve with cucumber and tomato on the side. Top with chives.
Use the smoked mackerel today — fridge priority item. Rich and filling winter breakfast.

Braised Pork with Courgette, Green Beans & Tomatoes
~26 g protein (Landi's portion)

Ingredients (×4)

  • Pork (from freezer — defrost today/Sunday), approx. 700–900 g
  • 4 large courgette, cut into thick rounds (2 cm) — use now, fridge priority
  • Large handful green beans, trimmed — use now, fridge priority
  • 1 tin (400 g) chopped tomatoes (pantry)
  • 1 large onion, chopped · 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 10 ml tomato sauce (pantry) · 5 ml olive oil
  • 5 g paprika · 5 g dried mixed herbs · salt and pepper

Method

  1. Brown pork in olive oil over medium-high heat, 3 min per side.
  2. Add onion and garlic, cook 2 min. Add chopped tomatoes, tomato sauce, paprika, herbs, salt and pepper.
  3. Cover and simmer on low 40–45 min until pork is very tender. Add a splash of water if drying out.
  4. Add courgette rounds and green beans in the last 10 minutes — tender but not mushy.
  5. Landi's plate: 100–120 g pork + 2–3 tbsp tomato sauce + courgette and green beans. No butter. Protein first.
Note for Mom: No cream or butter on Landi's portion. Add all the courgette and green beans to the pot — they need to be finished today.

Gochujang Chicken Rice Bowl
~27 g protein

Ingredients (1 serving)

  • 100 g boneless chicken thigh, cooked (see method below)
  • 60 g cooked short-grain Japanese rice
  • Sauce: 5 ml gochujang paste · 10 ml soy sauce · 5 ml mirin · 3–4 drops sesame oil · 5 ml rice vinegar
  • Cucumber slices + carrot ribbons (peeler on fridge carrots)
  • Chives, chopped

Method

  1. Cook chicken: Defrost 1–2 boneless thighs. Season lightly. Pan-fry in a dry non-stick pan over medium heat 4–5 min per side until cooked through. Slice into strips.
  2. Rice: Cook 60 g dry short-grain rice in Instant Pot (equal volume water, pressure 3 min, natural release 10 min). Or cook a larger batch and refrigerate.
  3. Sauce: Whisk gochujang, soy, mirin, sesame oil and rice vinegar together in a small bowl.
  4. Assemble: rice first in a bowl, chicken on top, drizzle sauce over, arrange cucumber and carrot alongside. Top with chives.
Gochujang heat level confirmed tolerable — enjoy! Adjust the amount of paste to taste.

Soy & Ginger Chicken Thighs with Roasted Butternut & Peas
~25 g protein (Landi's portion)

Ingredients (×4)

  • 8 skinless deboned chicken thighs (defrost Mon evening from pack of 8)
  • Marinade: 30 ml soy sauce · 20 ml mirin · 15 ml roasted garlic & onion puree (fridge) · 5 ml pureed ginger (fridge) · 5 ml sesame oil · 5 ml olive oil
  • 1 medium butternut squash, peeled, cubed (2–3 cm)
  • 1 can peas in brine, drained and heated
  • Any leftover courgette or green beans from Monday — add to roasting tray
  • Salt and pepper · olive oil for butternut

Method

  1. Mix marinade ingredients. Pour over chicken thighs. Marinate 30 minutes (or overnight in fridge).
  2. Preheat oven to 200 °C. Toss butternut cubes in olive oil, salt and pepper. Roast 25–30 min. Add any remaining courgette/green beans to the tray in the last 10 min.
  3. Place chicken thighs in a baking dish. Bake at 200 °C for 20–22 min until cooked through. Or air fry at 190 °C for 16–18 min.
  4. Heat peas in a saucepan or microwave 2 minutes. Drain.
  5. Landi's plate: 2 thighs (100–120 g) + 60 g butternut + 3 tbsp peas. Small portion rice optional. Protein first.
Note for Mom: No extra butter or sauce on Landi's portion. A spoonful of the marinade pan juices over Landi's chicken is fine.

Chinese Egg & Tomato Stir-Fry (番茄炒蛋) with Short-Grain Rice
~20 g protein

Ingredients (1 serving)

  • 2–3 large eggs, beaten well
  • 1 medium tomato (fridge), roughly chopped
  • 5 ml sesame oil
  • 10 ml soy sauce
  • ½ tsp sugar (or 1 small sachet Huletts EquiSweet)
  • 60 g cooked short-grain rice (cook fresh or use leftover)
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Beat eggs well with a pinch of salt.
  2. Heat sesame oil in a non-stick pan over medium-high. Pour in eggs — let set for 5 seconds, then scramble gently into large soft curds. Push to the side of the pan.
  3. Add tomato chunks to the empty side. Cook 1–2 min until tomato softens and releases juices.
  4. Mix together gently. Add soy sauce and sweetener. Toss to combine — another 30 seconds.
  5. Serve over warm rice. The tomato sauce mingles into the rice beautifully.
Classic Chinese home cooking. The tomato becomes silky and slightly sweet — deeply comforting on a winter morning. Total cook time under 5 minutes.

Chickpea & Tuna Soup
~23 g protein

Ingredients (1 serving)

  • 100 g canned chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 50 g canned tuna in brine, drained (half a can)
  • 400 ml chicken stock (pantry)
  • 1 carrot (fridge), diced small
  • ½ green pepper (fresh produce), diced
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced (fridge) · 5 ml pureed ginger (fridge)
  • 1 tsp curry powder · salt and pepper
  • 5 ml olive oil

Method

  1. Heat olive oil in a small pot. Sauté garlic, ginger, and curry powder 30 seconds until fragrant.
  2. Add carrot and green pepper. Cook 2 minutes.
  3. Pour in chicken stock. Add chickpeas. Simmer 8–10 minutes until carrot is tender.
  4. Stir in drained tuna — cook 1 minute more. Season to taste.
  5. Serve in a mug or small bowl. Protein first — eat the chickpeas and tuna before the broth.
Warm, filling, and high-fibre. Use the chickpea brine to cook the veg in — it adds a subtle flavour boost.

Miso Udon Bowl with Soft-Boiled Eggs
~18 g protein

Ingredients (1 serving)

  • 1 portion udon noodles (from pantry) — cook 60–70 g dry
  • 400 ml chicken stock (pantry)
  • 15 g white miso paste (fridge) — dissolve in a ladle of hot stock first

Method

  5. Pour miso broth over noodles. Place soft-boiled egg halves on top. Finish with soy sauce.
  6. Landi's portion: Small bowl — noodles first, broth, then egg. Eat eggs first.
Classic Friday comfort food. Japanese miso broth with eggs is easy on the stomach and deeply warming.

Steamed Pot Stickers & Lamb Dumplings with Gochujang-Ponzu Dipping Sauce
~25 g protein (Landi's portion)

Ingredients (×2)

  • Chili sautéed chicken pot stickers (from freezer) — defrost from fridge overnight (Fri evening)
  • Lamb with spring onion dumplings (from freezer) — defrost from fridge overnight
  • Dipping sauce: 5 ml gochujang paste · 20 ml ponzu sauce · 5 ml rice vinegar · 5 ml soy sauce · 2–3 drops sesame oil
  • 200 ml water (for Instant Pot steaming)
  • Optional garnish: cucumber slices, chives, carrot ribbons

Method

  1. Dipping sauce: Whisk gochujang, ponzu, rice vinegar, soy and sesame oil together in a small bowl. Taste — add more ponzu if too spicy, more gochujang if you want more heat.
  2. Instant Pot — steam method: Add 200 ml water to the pot. Place steamer basket or trivet inside. Arrange dumplings and pot stickers in a single layer (don't stack).
  3. Steam on HIGH pressure for 5 minutes. Quick release. Check that filling is piping hot through.
  4. Alternatively, steam in a bamboo steamer over a pot of simmering water for 8–10 minutes.
  5. Landi's portion: 5–7 pieces total (small SADI-S portion) — mix of both types. Protein first before any dipping sauce-heavy bites.
Why steam instead of fry? The label says "chili sautéed" but steaming keeps the fat content much lower — important for post-bariatric eating. The dipping sauce provides all the flavour you need. Gochujang heat confirmed — enjoy!
