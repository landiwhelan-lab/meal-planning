Protein Scrambled Eggs with Cottage Cheese, Cucumber & Tomato
~21 g protein (Landi)

Ingredients (1 serving)

  • 2 large eggs
  • 50 g low-fat smooth cottage cheese (stirred directly into beaten eggs)
  • 5 ml olive oil
  • Cucumber slices + 2–3 slices tomato (fridge)
  • 2 Provita Maize Crackerbread (on the side)
  • Black pepper · fresh chives if available

Method

  1. Beat eggs well. Stir cottage cheese into the beaten eggs — lumpy is fine, it melts smooth.
  2. Heat olive oil in a non-stick pan over low-medium heat. Add egg and cottage cheese mixture.
  3. Stir slowly with a spatula. The cottage cheese doubles the protein with almost no change in volume — great for small bariatric portions.
  4. Remove from heat when just set. Serve with cucumber, tomato, and crackerbreads on the side.
  5. Protein first: eat the eggs before the crackerbreads.

Red Miso & Tuna Udon Soup
~17 g protein (Landi)

Ingredients (1 serving)

  • 60 g dry udon noodles (cook per packet, usually 3–4 min in boiling water)
  • 350 ml chicken or bone broth (stock cube or bone broth concentrate in hot water)
  • 15 g red miso paste (fridge) — dissolved separately in a ladle of hot broth
  • 60 g canned tuna in brine, drained (about half a standard can — share 1 can between 2 for lunch)
  • 5 ml pureed ginger (fridge) · 5 ml soy sauce · 3–4 drops sesame oil
  • Cucumber slices or a few carrot ribbons on the side

Method

  1. Bring broth to a simmer. Add ginger. Simmer 2 minutes.
  2. Cook udon noodles separately in boiling water per packet. Drain and add to bowl.
  3. Dissolve red miso in a ladle of the hot broth first, then stir back into the pot. Do not boil after adding miso.
  4. Pour miso broth over noodles. Top with drained tuna, drizzle soy and sesame oil.
  5. Landi's portion: small bowl — eat tuna first, then noodles, sip broth last.
Use red miso today for a deeper, more robust flavour — great on a winter Monday. Save white miso for later in the week.

Warthog Mince Bolognese with Penne
~28 g protein (Landi's portion)

Ingredients (×4)

  • 1 × 500 g warthog mince (defrost Sun evening)
  • Penne pasta (pantry) — cook 320 g dry for the family; Landi's portion: 60 g dry = ~150 g cooked
  • 3 medium tomatoes (fridge), roughly chopped — or 1 tin chopped tomatoes if preferred
  • 1 green pepper (to buy), deseeded and diced
  • 2 medium onions, diced (pantry)
  • 4 cloves fresh garlic, minced (fridge)
  • 1 beef stock cube dissolved in 100 ml hot water
  • 5 ml olive oil · 5 g dried mixed herbs (oregano, basil) · salt and pepper
  • Optional: 15 ml tomato paste if available, or 10 ml onion sauce (pantry)

Method

  1. Heat olive oil in a large pot over medium-high heat. Brown the warthog mince in batches, breaking up well. Do not crowd the pan. Set aside.
  2. In the same pot, sauté onion 3–4 min until soft. Add garlic and green pepper, cook 2 min more.
  3. Return mince to the pot. Add chopped tomatoes, beef stock, herbs, salt and pepper. Stir well.
  4. Cover and simmer on low heat for 25–30 min, stirring occasionally. Add a splash of water if it thickens too much.
  5. Cook penne in a separate pot of salted boiling water until al dente. Drain.
  6. Landi's plate: 100–120 g mince sauce (weigh the mince portion) + 60 g cooked penne. No added cheese or cream on Landi's portion. Protein first — eat the mince before the pasta.
Note for Mom: No cream or cheese on Landi's portion. Warthog is leaner than beef — it will cook slightly faster. The green pepper must go in — it is good for Landi.

Boerewors with Gem Squash & Butter Beans in Curry Sauce
~22 g protein (Landi's portion)

Ingredients (×4)

  • Boerewors (from freezer — defrost Mon evening), approx. 600–700 g

  5. Landi's plate: 100 g boerewors + 1 gem squash half (scoop out the flesh and eat it) + 4 heaped tbsp butter beans in curry sauce. No rice for Landi unless she wants a very small scoop. Protein first.
Note for Mom: Boerewors has some fat — keep Landi's portion to 100 g. The gem squash flesh is soft and easy to eat — encourage her to scoop it all out. No butter in Landi's squash.

Chinese Tomato & Egg Stir-Fry (番茄炒蛋) with Short-Grain Rice
~20 g protein (Landi)

Ingredients (1 serving)

  • 3 large eggs, beaten well
  • 1 medium tomato (fridge), roughly chopped into 6–8 pieces
  • 5 ml sesame oil
  • 10 ml soy sauce
  • 1 Huletts EquiSweet sachet (or pinch regular sugar — small amount only)
  • 60 g cooked short-grain Japanese rice (small winter scoop)
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Beat 3 eggs well with a pinch of salt.
  2. Heat sesame oil in a non-stick pan over medium-high. Pour in eggs — let set for 5 sec, then scramble gently into large soft curds. Push to the side.
  3. Add tomato to the empty side. Cook 1–2 min until tomato softens and releases juices.
  4. Mix gently. Add soy sauce and sweetener. Toss together — another 30 seconds off heat.
  5. Serve over warm rice. Total cook time under 5 minutes.
  6. Eat eggs and tomato before finishing the rice.
Classic Chinese home breakfast. The tomato becomes silky as the juices mingle into the egg. Rich, savoury, 5 minutes.

Air-Fried Chicken Steaklets with Soft Polenta & Roasted Butternut
~27 g protein (Landi's portion)

Ingredients (×4)

  • Crumbled chicken steaklets (from freezer — defrost Tue evening), approx. 600–800 g
  • 1 medium butternut squash (pantry), peeled, deseeded, cubed into 2 cm pieces
  • 3–4 carrots (fridge), peeled and cut into thick batons
  • Polenta: 200 g dry polenta (pantry) + 800 ml chicken stock (stock cubes) — for soft creamy polenta
  • Olive oil for roasting veg · salt and pepper · paprika or dried herbs for butternut

Method

  1. Roasted butternut & carrots: Preheat oven to 200 °C. Toss butternut cubes and carrot batons in olive oil, salt, pepper, and a pinch of paprika. Spread on a baking tray. Roast 25–30 min until golden and tender.
  2. Chicken steaklets — air fryer: Place in air fryer basket in a single layer (do in batches if needed). Air fry at 190 °C for 14–16 min until golden and crispy, turning halfway. Check packaging instructions — most crumbed steaklets take this range.
  3. Soft polenta: Bring 800 ml chicken stock to a simmer in a medium pot. Pour in polenta in a thin stream, whisking constantly. Reduce heat to low. Stir continuously for 5–7 min until thick and smooth. Season with salt and pepper. Add a splash more stock if it thickens too much.
  4. Landi's plate: 1–2 chicken steaklets (120 g total) + 80 g soft polenta + 80 g roasted butternut + a few carrot batons. Protein first — eat the chicken before the polenta. Small polenta portion for Landi (it is filling).
Note for Mom: No butter in Landi's polenta — use chicken stock only. Soft polenta is gentle on the stomach and easy to eat. A small portion for Landi is fine — the chicken is the priority. Use any leftover roasted butternut for Landi's lunch or snack Thursday.

Warthog Mince Cottage Pie with Sweet Potato Mash
~27 g protein (Landi's portion)

Ingredients (×4)

  • 1 × 500 g warthog mince (defrost Wed evening)
  • 2 medium onions, diced · 3–4 carrots (fridge), diced
  • 1 can baked beans (pantry) — adds bulk and a little sweetness to the filling
  • 1 beef stock cube dissolved in 150 ml hot water
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced · 5 ml olive oil · salt and pepper · 5 g dried thyme
  • Topping: 2 large sweet potatoes (1 in stock + 1 to buy), peeled, cubed, boiled then mashed with olive oil, salt and pepper (no butter for Landi's portion)

Method

  1. Filling: Heat olive oil in a large oven-safe pot or deep pan. Brown warthog mince, breaking it up well. Remove and set aside. Sauté onion, carrot, and garlic in the same pot for 4–5 min. Return mince. Add beef stock, baked beans, thyme, salt and pepper. Simmer on low for 15–20 min until carrots are tender and sauce has reduced slightly.
  2. Sweet potato mash: Peel and cube sweet potatoes. Boil in salted water 12–15 min until very tender. Drain and mash with olive oil (not butter — for Landi), salt and pepper until smooth.
  3. Transfer filling to a baking dish (or leave in the oven-safe pot). Spread sweet potato mash evenly over the top.
  4. Bake at 190 °C for 20–25 min until the mash is lightly golden on top and the filling is bubbling at the edges.
  5. Landi's portion: 100–120 g mince filling + 80 g sweet potato mash topping. Mince first.
Note for Mom: No butter in Landi's sweet potato mash — olive oil only. Warthog mince is very lean, so keep an eye on the filling — it should be saucy, not dry. Add a splash of water or stock if it looks dry before baking.

Oven-Baked Snoek with Roasted Sweet Potato & Carrots
~30 g protein (Landi's portion)

Ingredients (×4)

  • Snoek (from freezer — defrost Thu evening), whole or fillets — approx. 800 g–1 kg
  • Basting: 30 ml olive oil · 4 cloves garlic, minced · juice of 1 lemon · 5 g dried mixed herbs (thyme, rosemary) · salt and black pepper
  • 2 large sweet potatoes (to buy, or use any remaining from Thu) — peeled and sliced into 1.5 cm rounds
  • 4 large carrots (fridge), peeled and cut into thick batons

Method

  4. Landi's plate: 150 g snoek (flaked, no skin) + 80 g roasted sweet potato + 3–4 carrot batons. Protein first — fish before the veg.
Note for Mom: Snoek is a high-protein, omega-3-rich fish — excellent for Landi. The traditional apricot jam basting is off the menu (too much sugar) but the garlic and lemon version is delicious. No butter on the fish for Landi's portion.

Steamed Pot Stickers & BBQ Pork Bao Buns with Udon Broth & Veg Sides
~28 g protein (Landi's portion)

Proteins (×4)

  • 10 pot stickers (chilli sautéed chicken — from freezer, defrost Fri evening) — approximately 2–3 each

  • 4 portions udon noodles (pantry) — cook 60 g dry per person in boiling water 3–4 min, drain
  • 1.2 L chicken stock (stock cubes or bone broth concentrate + boiling water)
  • 20 ml soy sauce · 10 ml mirin · 10 ml pureed ginger (fridge) · 5 ml sesame oil
  • Optional: a spoonful of white miso dissolved in the broth

Veg Sides

  • 1 cucumber, cut into batons
  • 3–4 carrots (fridge), cut into batons or thin slices

  • Ponzu: Ponzu sauce direct from bottle (fridge)
  • Gochujang: 10 ml gochujang · 20 ml ponzu · 5 ml rice vinegar · 3–4 drops sesame oil — whisk together

Method

  1. Dipping sauces: Prepare both sauces and pour into small dipping bowls. Set the table with sauces, cucumber batons, and carrot sticks.
  2. Udon broth: Bring stock to a simmer. Add soy, mirin, ginger, sesame oil (and miso if using). Simmer 5 min. Keep warm on the hob.
  3. Udon noodles: Cook in a separate pot of boiling water per packet. Drain and portion into bowls. Ladle hot broth over. Serve as the base alongside the dumplings.
  4. Pot stickers — Instant Pot steam: Add 200 ml water to the Instant Pot. Place trivet inside. Arrange pot stickers in a single layer. Steam on HIGH pressure 5 min, quick release. Check filling is piping hot.
  5. Bao buns — steam: Steam in the Instant Pot (same method) or in a bamboo steamer over simmering water for 8–10 min from defrosted. Bao buns should be puffy and soft.
  6. Serve everything at the table together — feast style.
  7. Landi's portion: 2–3 pot stickers + 1 bao bun + small bowl udon broth with a few noodles. Eat protein first (dumplings, bao pork filling). Use dipping sauces freely. Eat slowly — chew very well. Leave the cucumber and carrot batons out on the table for grazing.
Tip: Steam the pot stickers rather than pan-frying — the dipping sauce gives all the flavour, and steaming keeps fat content low. Gochujang dipping sauce is the winner with the pot stickers. Ponzu is great with the bao bun.

Dinner · ~18:30
Instant Pot Lamb Knuckle with Green Beans & Roasted Carrots
~20 g protein (Landi's portion)*

Ingredients (×4)

  • 1 lamb knuckle (from freezer — defrost Sat evening)
  • 1 onion, roughly chopped · 3 cloves garlic, whole · 1 lamb stock cube dissolved in 250 ml hot water
  • 5 g dried rosemary · 5 g dried thyme · salt and black pepper
  • 200 ml water (for pressure cooking)
  • Sides: Green beans (to buy) — trimmed, blanched or steamed
  • 4–5 carrots (fridge), peeled and cut into thick batons — roast in oven
  • Optional: any remaining sweet potato from the week, cubed and roasted alongside the carrots

Method

  1. Roasted carrots: Preheat oven to 200 °C. Toss carrot batons (and sweet potato if using) in olive oil, salt and pepper. Roast 25–30 min. Can go into the oven while the lamb pressure-cooks.
  2. Lamb knuckle — Instant Pot: Place onion, garlic, rosemary, and thyme at the bottom of the Instant Pot. Season the lamb knuckle well with salt and pepper and sit it on top. Pour over lamb stock and 200 ml water. Seal lid.
  3. Pressure cook on HIGH for 50 minutes. Allow natural pressure release for 15 minutes, then quick release any remaining steam.
  4. The meat should be completely fall-off-the-bone tender. Shred the meat off the bone with two forks. Discard the bone. Skim any visible fat off the cooking juices and use the juices as a light sauce.
  5. Green beans: Blanch in boiling salted water for 3–4 min until just tender but still green. Or steam for 5 min. Season with olive oil, salt and pepper.
  6. Landi's plate: 100–120 g shredded lamb + a ladle of cooking juices + green beans + roasted carrots. Protein first.
* Lamb knuckle note: One knuckle is bone-heavy — actual meat yield for 4 people will be smaller portions. This is fine for Landi's SADI-S portion sizes. If Landi's protein feels low at the end of the day, add 1–2 hard-boiled eggs or a lamb liver cake alongside dinner as a top-up. The cooking juices from the Instant Pot are flavour-forward (not protein-significant) so don't count them as protein.
