Batch Prep Guide

🥚 Hard-Boiled Eggs — Batch of 8 (do Saturdays)

Boil on Saturday morning. Covers afternoon snacks for ~4 days. Store unpeeled in fridge.

Method

  1. Place 8 eggs in a pot, cover with cold water by 2 cm.
  2. Bring to a boil, reduce to gentle simmer, cook 10 minutes.
  3. Transfer to iced water for 5 minutes. Store unpeeled in a sealed container in fridge.
  4. Per serving: peel 2 eggs, halve, drizzle 5 ml soy sauce + pinch sesame seeds.

🍖 Air-Fryer Liver Cakes — Mom makes Saturday 30 May

Batch of 12–16. Freeze in batches of 4 wrapped in foil. Reheat from frozen at 160 °C for 6–8 min.

Ingredients

  • 500 g minced lamb liver
  • 1 medium potato (approx. 150 g), boiled and mashed
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 medium onion, finely grated
  • 125 g self-raising flour
  • Salt, pepper, paprika to taste
  • Light spray of olive oil for air-fryer basket

Method

  1. Mix all ingredients well. Mixture should be thick enough to hold shape — add flour if too wet.
  2. Shape into flat patties ~1.5 cm thick (approx. 50–60 g each).
  3. Preheat air fryer to 180 °C. Spray basket lightly with olive oil.
  4. Cook in single layer for 10–12 minutes, flipping halfway. Golden outside, cooked through.
  5. Cool on a rack. Freeze in batches of 4.
~12–14 g protein per cake. Excellent iron source. Reheat from frozen at 160 °C for 6–8 min.

Chawanmushi — Japanese Steamed Egg Custard
~22 g protein

Ingredients (1 serving)

  • 3 large eggs
  • 250 ml warm dashi (5 g Hondashi + 250 ml warm water)
  • 10 ml Kikkoman soy sauce
  • 50 g silken tofu, small cubes (buy this weekend)
  • 30 g cooked chicken or 1 extra egg if no chicken
  • 1 spring onion, sliced

Method (Instant Pot)

  1. Whisk eggs gently — no foam. Strain through a sieve.
  2. Mix in warm dashi and soy. Pour into a heatproof ramekin or bowl. Add tofu and chicken pieces. Cover tightly with foil.
  3. Add 300 ml water to Instant Pot. Place trivet inside. Put ramekin on trivet.
  4. Steam on LOW pressure for 10 minutes. Quick release. Centre should be just set and slightly wobbly.
  5. Top with spring onion. Eat with a spoon.

Oyakodon — Chicken & Egg Rice Bowl
~30 g protein (Landi's portion)

Ingredients (×2)

  • 300 g boneless chicken thighs, cut into 3 cm pieces
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1 medium onion (about 120 g), thinly sliced
  • 200 ml dashi (10 g Hondashi + 200 ml water)
  • 30 ml Kikkoman soy sauce
  • 30 ml mirin
  • 10 g sugar (or 1 Huletts EquiSweet sachet to reduce sugar)
  • 200 g dry short-grain rice, cooked
  • 2 spring onions, sliced

Method

  1. Cook rice in Instant Pot: rinse, equal volume water, pressure cook 3 min, natural release 10 min.
  2. In a wide pan, combine dashi, soy, mirin and sugar. Bring to a simmer. Add onion slices, cook 3 minutes.
  3. Add chicken pieces. Simmer 5–6 minutes until just cooked through.
  4. Beat eggs lightly (leave slightly streaky). Pour over chicken in a circular motion. Cover and cook on LOW heat for 90 seconds — egg should be softly set, slightly runny in places.
  5. Slide immediately over rice bowls. Top with spring onion.
  6. Landi's bowl: 60 g rice + 100 g chicken pieces + egg. Protein first.
Note: The egg should be slightly runny on top — this is correct for oyakodon. Cook longer if you prefer fully set egg.

Scrambled Eggs with Smoked Mackerel & Rye Crispbread
~28 g protein

Ingredients (1 serving)

  • 3 large eggs
  • 30 ml lactose-free milk
  • 60 g smoked mackerel, flaked
  • Small knob (5 ml) olive oil
  • 2 Provita Maize Crackerbread
  • Black pepper + chives or spring onion

Method

  1. Whisk eggs with milk, season well.
  2. Heat olive oil in a non-stick pan over low-medium heat. Add eggs. Stir slowly and continuously with a spatula — low and slow makes them creamy.
  3. Just before fully set, fold in flaked mackerel. Remove from heat immediately (residual heat finishes them).
  4. Serve on Provita crackerbreads. Top with pepper and spring onion.
Scandinavian-style breakfast. Rich, creamy, warm — good for winter mornings.

Slow-Cooked Pork Neck in Tomato & Herb Sauce with Gem Squash
~26 g protein (Landi's portion)

Pan-Seared Duck Breast with Mirin-Soy Pan Sauce & Roasted Root Vegetables
~30 g protein (Landi's portion)

Ingredients (×2)

  • 2 duck breasts (approx. 350–400 g total)
  • Pan sauce: 30 ml mirin + 20 ml Kikkoman soy sauce + 10 ml rice wine vinegar + 1 garlic clove minced + 1 cm fresh ginger, grated (or pinch ground ginger)
  • 300 g mixed root veg: parsnip, carrot, sweet potato — peeled and cut into 3 cm chunks
  • 15 ml olive oil for veg
  • 5 g dried thyme · salt and pepper
  • Spring onion or fresh chives to garnish

Method

  1. Preheat oven to 200 °C. Toss root veg with olive oil, thyme, salt and pepper. Roast 30–35 minutes until golden and tender.
  2. Score duck skin in a crosshatch pattern (cuts through fat, not into flesh). Season generously with salt and pepper.
  3. Place duck breasts skin-side DOWN in a cold, dry oven-safe pan. Turn heat to medium. Cook 8–10 minutes — the fat renders slowly and the skin becomes deeply golden and crisp. Pour off excess fat as it renders.
  4. Flip duck. Transfer pan to oven. Roast 6–8 minutes for medium-pink inside. Rest 5 minutes before slicing.
  5. While duck rests, make pan sauce: pour off most remaining fat from pan, leaving just a film. Return to heat, add garlic and ginger — sizzle 30 seconds. Add mirin, soy and vinegar. Simmer 2 minutes until slightly reduced and glossy.
  6. Landi's plate: 100–120 g sliced duck breast (skin on or off — your choice) + 80 g roasted veg. Spoon sauce over. Protein first.
Duck tip: Starting in a cold pan renders the fat slowly — far better than hot pan. Don't rush this step.
